What is e-waste?

Electronic waste, often called e-waste, means electrical or electronic equipment that has been discarded because it is old, broken, unwanted or no longer useful to its owner.

Common examples

  • Mobile phones and chargers
  • Laptops, keyboards and computer mice
  • Televisions and monitors
  • Printers and scanners
  • Headphones, cables and small appliances
  • Electronic toys and other battery-powered devices

How has e-waste changed over time?

As computers, mobile phones and household electronics became more common, the amount of discarded equipment grew. New models, changing technology and repairs that can be difficult or expensive all contribute to shorter useful lives for some devices.

Today, e-waste is a global issue. The Global E-waste Monitor estimated 62 million tonnes were generated in 2022, with 22.3% documented as formally collected and recycled. The report projects 82 million tonnes by 2030 if current trends continue.

In India, e-waste comes from homes, offices, schools, shops and industries. For the latest national figures and rules, check official Central Pollution Control Board resources rather than relying on old statistics.
