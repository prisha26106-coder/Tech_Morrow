Environmental impact

When e-waste is dumped, burned or taken apart without proper protection, it can harm ecosystems and the people who live and work nearby.

Land and soil

Unsafe dismantling and dumping can release lead and other hazardous substances into soil. Contamination can affect plants, animals and nearby communities.

Water

Pollutants from poorly managed waste can enter drains, rivers and groundwater. This can affect aquatic life and the quality of water used by people.

Air

Burning cables and electronic parts can release harmful smoke and fine particles. E-waste should never be burned to recover metals.

Wildlife and human health

Exposure to hazardous substances can affect health, especially for workers and children near informal recycling sites. Safe equipment, proper treatment and authorized recycling routes reduce these risks.

Case study: informal e-waste processing

Agbogbloshie in Accra, Ghana, has often been discussed in research and media about informal electronic-waste processing. Reports have documented environmental and worker-safety concerns associated with unsafe dismantling and burning. Conditions can change over time, so use current research when presenting this case study.
